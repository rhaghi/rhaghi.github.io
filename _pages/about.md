I am a Postdoctoral Scholar at the Tufts Institute for AI within the School of Engineering, with a joint appointment between the Tufts Institute for AI and the Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering. My research focuses on physics-aware machine learning for infrastructure systems, with particular emphasis on offshore wind turbine modeling, digital twins, and structural dynamics.

## Research Interests

My work lies at the intersection of machine learning and physical systems, developing data-driven approaches for wind energy applications. My research areas include:

* **Neural Operators and Surrogate Models**: Developing physics-informed neural networks and Fourier Neural Operators for structural dynamics and wind turbine modeling
* **Offshore Wind Energy**: Multiphysics modeling, validation, and analysis of utility-scale operational offshore wind turbines
* **Structural Dynamics**: Model calibration, uncertainty quantification, and system identification for large-scale infrastructure systems
* **Digital Twin Development**: Creating virtual sensors and data-driven models for wind turbine design and maintenance

## Background

Prior to joining Tufts, I earned my Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering from the University of Victoria (2017-2024), where I developed data-driven surrogate models for wind turbine design and maintenance applications under the supervision of Dr. Curran Crawford. My doctoral research combined uncertainty quantification, machine learning, and structural dynamics to create computationally efficient models for wind energy systems.

Before pursuing my doctorate, I spent six years (2011-2017) as an Offshore Wind Loads Engineer at Siemens Gamesa Renewables in The Hague, Netherlands, where I calculated, monitored, and validated loads on offshore wind turbines based on IEC and DNV standards. This industry experience provided me with deep practical knowledge of wind turbine design, certification processes, and operational challenges that continues to inform my research.

I hold an M.Sc. in Mechanical Engineering from Delft University of Technology (2011), where my thesis focused on the integrated design and optimization of offshore wind turbine monopile support structures, and an M.Sc. in Automotive Engineering from Kingston University (2006).

## Recent Work

My current research involves training neural operators on wind turbine operational data, developing uncertainty quantification methods for offshore wind turbine models, and analyzing wake effects in offshore wind farms. I have published in leading journals including Wind Energy Science, Renewable Energy, and Energies, and regularly present at major conferences such as NAWEA, WCCM, and Wind Energy Science Conference.

## Outreach and Service

I am committed to science education and outreach. I created and conduct "Megawatts & Marbles," an interactive educational game that teaches K-12 students about energy systems through gamification of the grid system. I also serve as a science fair judge and have mentored graduate students, predominantly women, throughout my academic career.
